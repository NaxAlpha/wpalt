# Business workflows — M4 development

M4 is in progress in PR #8. The current working slices provide form design/publication/collection, protected response viewing, purpose-specific confirmed subscriptions, structured campaign composition, a durable local/SMTP outbox and private draft recovery. It does not yet deliver the complete business/audience contract.

When `business_enabled = true` (default), editors and administrators open **Forms**, create a titled form, edit labels and text/number/checkbox inputs, mark required fields, arrange up to eight ordered steps and add conditions or numeric sum/product calculations from earlier fields. Save the working copy, then publish when ready. Publication preserves its reusable groups; later shared-schema edits do not reinterpret accepted responses. The published form is available at `/forms/<id>`. Responses are visible through **View responses** in the same admin workflow. Moderator accounts and unauthenticated visitors cannot read or edit private business state.

The designer creates primitive fields and bounded nested groups/repeaters, email/long-text/choice widgets, scores and typed-name acknowledgment signatures. Signatures preserve a published statement and explicit acceptance with the immutable response; they do not establish legal identity or supply a cryptographic signing certificate. Embedded content/theme blocks, exact pricing and moderated registration/content actions remain pending. Arithmetic currently uses finite floating-point values; it is not a payment or exact-currency engine. Audience/communications/engagement remain required M4 work, not removed scope.

The response limit defaults to 10,000 accepted entries per form, across its publications, and may be configured between 1 and 1,000,000 in the designer. Concurrent submissions cannot exceed the limit. Repeating an accepted UUID/payload returns the same entry without consuming another slot; changing the payload for that identifier conflicts. New responses using a stale public version are rejected. This storage bound is not a complete spam or identity-verification system; additional form abuse controls are pending.

Definitions allow at most 32 top-level fields, eight ordered steps, the shared depth/width/expanded-node budgets, and earlier-field rule dependencies. Collection requests are capped at 128 KiB, individual strings at 8,000 bytes and repeaters by their declared limits (at most 50). Hidden values are discarded and calculated values recomputed on the server before storage. Private server drafts use a random hashed form-scoped capability, revision compare-and-swap, seven-day expiry and a 1,000-live-draft limit per form. Partial saves cannot trigger contact or mail actions. A republished form requires deliberate review of an older draft.

The designer and public form need JavaScript, served from the same self-contained executable. No external editor/validation service or wpalt account is required. Failed requests preserve the values in the currently open page; device recovery is opt-in, bounded and expires after seven days of inactivity. Existing open forms can be edited without a connection; an interrupted submission retries its original identifier and payload. This does not yet provide fresh offline page navigation. Debug records include request correlation, form/entry identifiers and publication versions, never form payloads.

Set `business_enabled = false` in the TOML configuration, or `WPALT_BUSINESS_ENABLED=false`, and restart to remove business routes, navigation and the form asset. Stored data remains available for a later re-enable and for backups. Mail/campaign workers use the scheduler; analytics capture remains pending. See [operations](operations.md#m4-development-upgrade-in-progress) for schema-6/backup-v6 recovery. Schema 6 is an evolving pre-adoption branch format: disposable data made by intermediate M4 development commits may require reset/reseed; meaningful M3.5 schema-4 data follows the documented upgrade/independent-backup path.


## Audience and communications

Open **Audience** to create a list with its explicit purpose and policy version. In the form designer choose the list, an email text field and an initially unchecked consent checkbox. Publication preserves the purpose; accepted consent creates a pending membership and one confirmation message transactionally. Duplicate or concurrent requests cannot enqueue repeated confirmations during its 24-hour validity. Recipient confirmation requires deliberate POST; a mail scanner opening GET does not subscribe. A withdrawn membership cannot be silently reactivated by an anonymous claim. Current-policy confirmed membership is required for campaigns.

Open **Campaigns** from Audience to compose with the same structured editor used for posts. Save a draft, schedule with a UTC epoch timestamp, or send now. Expansion locks one campaign and admits at most 50 recipients per transaction, using contact-ID keyset traversal and a confirmation cutoff. Delivery checks current consent again. Contacts provide bounded typed company/source/score attributes and versioned owner updates. Campaign segments combine exact company/source matches and a minimum score. Conditional notification/automation controls and wider recipient/report pagination remain required work.

`[mail]` configuration defaults to `enabled = true`, `from = "site@localhost.test"`, `max_attempts = 5` and no SMTP connections. The default writes deterministic private `.eml` files under `data_dir/outbox`; spool success is not inbox delivery. `[[mail.smtp]]` accepts `host`, `port`, `tls` (`starttls`, `tls` or loopback-IP-only `local`), `username` and `password`, at most four owner-chosen routes. Configure a valid sender and owner-operated MTA for real delivery. No external account is required. SMTP TLS uses the bundled WebPKI trust roots. Configuration output redacts usernames/passwords and database credentials.

**Mail** records states and bounded attempt history. A deliberate retry of sent/spooled/uncertain mail requires acknowledgment of duplicate-delivery risk. Expired leases and lost SMTP receipts become uncertain; they are never automatically replayed. Temporary negative responses or TLS setup failures can retry/fall back; unclassified network failures remain conservatively uncertain. This does not claim exactly-once internet delivery, inbox reputation, DKIM signing or RFC8058 one-click compliance. Development tests communicate only with an owned loopback fixture.

Backup-v6 includes contacts, purposes, memberships, consent records, campaigns, withdrawal capabilities, message bodies/attempts and drafts. Fresh restore marks outstanding network leases uncertain and schedules local spools for reconstruction from stable bytes. Debug logging includes identifiers, outcomes and attempts, never recipients, capabilities, form values, mail bodies or SMTP credentials. Capability screens use `no-store`, `noindex` and `no-referrer`.


## Private responses and attachments

Choose **Protected attachment** presentation on a text field. Files are limited to the smaller of `max_upload_bytes` and 2 MiB. Supported types are UTF-8 plain text, PDF with its signature, and decoded PNG/JPEG/WebP images re-encoded as PNG with dimensions/allocation bounded to 4,096 pixels / 64 MiB. MIME headers alone are not accepted as image validation. PDF bytes are not malware scanned or disarmed; treat downloaded documents accordingly. A form admits at most 64 MiB / 1,000 files pending and accepted combined; global admission/expired-file retention optimization remains pending. UUID `.dat` storage is separate from public media and uses 0600 files. Upload capabilities expire after 24 hours and attach once, to the matching form/version/field. A successful submission stores only the attachment ID. A repeated accepted request returns the same entry without reattaching.

Response pages now provide FTS5 (SQLite) / GIN (PostgreSQL) token search, descending date/ID keyset pages of 40, versioned private notes and active-editor assignments. CSV exports use ascending ID pages of 500 (`?after=<last-id>`); visitor text is CSV quoted and formula-leading cells guarded. Entry and attachment access stays authenticated; backup-v6 includes private bytes, integrity metadata, follow-up state and derived search.

A contact detail page offers a bounded JSON export (up to 500 consent events), suppression and deliberate audience deletion. Deletion removes the contact, memberships, consent records and associated queue/spool data. An owner-keyed HMAC address guard blocks anonymous recreation, including concurrent submissions. The key and digests are included in the private backup. Independent form responses and historical backups have their own retention scope. Network messages already accepted by an SMTP peer cannot be recalled.
